PROJECT 2 — INTERVIEW NOTES

Phosphoproteomics measures phosphorylation sites on proteins and is useful for studying signaling and regulation.
A phosphosite is a modified residue at a defined position, e.g. EGFR_Y1068.
The main residues are serine, threonine and tyrosine.
Site localization asks whether fragment evidence supports the exact modified residue.
PTM = post-translational modification.
A phosphosite can change even when total protein abundance does not because phosphorylation reflects regulatory state.
FDR corrects for the large number of simultaneous statistical tests.
For interpretation, consider effect size, FDR, localization confidence, total protein context, biological replication and pathways.
